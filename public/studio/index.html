How the calculations work

Ranking and probability error

AUROC uses trapezoidal integration of the empirical ROC curve with tied scores grouped together. Average precision uses a step-weighted precision–recall sum, not trapezoidal PR area. The Brier score is the mean squared probability error.

Calibration

Plots use equal-width bins. The detailed plot shows 95% Wilson intervals for observed proportions. The calibration slope comes from logistic regression of outcomes on log-odds of predicted risk; calibration-in-the-large fits an intercept with that log-odds as an offset. The ideal values are 1 and 0, respectively. Fits that fail to converge are not reported. Probabilities at 0 or 1 are clipped to 10⁻¹² and 1−10⁻¹² only for log-based calculations.

Decision curves

Net benefit = TP/n − FP/n × t/(1−t), where t is the chosen probability threshold. Predictions equal to the threshold count as positive. The display multiplies net benefit by 100. Thresholds should reflect the intended clinical decision; this app does not select an optimal threshold.

Uncertainty

The bootstrap samples independent rows with replacement and evaluates every model on the same sampled rows. It reports 95% percentile intervals for AUROC, average precision, Brier score, and paired AUROC differences. Single-class resamples are skipped and counted. Predictions are held fixed: these intervals do not include model development uncertainty or correct training-set optimism.

Before interpreting a result

  • For an actual validation study, predictions must come from a genuinely independent evaluation dataset or a correctly designed resampling procedure. The built-in examples do not establish any real model’s performance.
  • This edition handles binary outcomes at a common horizon. It does not handle censoring, survey weights, repeated measures, or clustered bootstrap resampling.
  • For pooled cross-validation predictions, row bootstrap intervals can miss dependence from shared training folds. Use a study-appropriate evaluation method.
  • Small samples, very few events, and separated calibration fits can give unstable estimates. Subgroup differences here are descriptive.
  • Examples are entirely synthetic. The app supports research and education; its outputs are not a clinical deployment approval.

Privacy and sharing

Built-in examples are synthetic. Uploaded datasets are saved to this site's database only when you choose Upload & save. Saved datasets and preprocessing results are scoped to your signed-in account. Selecting a file alone does not upload it.

Dataset rows are never embedded in example links or published source packages. Downloaded prepared data and preprocessing recipes can contain values and category labels from your data; review them before sharing. No AI API keys are required.
